Key Stories

Weekend calendar is the first read

Palisadian-Post’s weekend picks include Pali High JV football at 4 p.m. and varsity football at 7 p.m. today at Stadium by the Sea, plus Theatre Palisades’ “Kimberly Akimbo” at Odyssey Theatre tonight and Saturday at 7:30 p.m. and Sunday at 2 p.m.

Saturday is the heaviest action day: Palisades Recovery Coalition’s Community Recovery Lab, Alphabets Neighborhood Cleanup, Beach Spirit Walk, El Medio brush-clearance day and Foothill Catalog Foundation design showcase are all listed.

PPCC election field narrows after candidate forum

At the PPCC candidate forum, three candidates withdrew: Kate Santoro and Martin Hak in Area 1, and Kimberly Bloom in Area 5. The article says Bart Young, May Sung, Lorie Cudzil, Sharon Kilbride and Andrew Wolfberg will represent Areas 1, 5, 6, 7 and 8, respectively.

Four races remain contested: at-large, Area 2, Area 3 and Area 4. Candidate answers focused heavily on safe rebuilding, water capacity, evacuation planning, construction management, truck routes, business restoration and support for returning residents.

Pali High returns to campus for fall

Circling The News reports that students are back at Palisades Charter High School after more than a year and a half of disruption. Students quoted in the piece compared the Palisades campus with the Sears building, with some welcoming the return and others noting tradeoffs.

The practical note: temporary replacement classrooms are on the baseball field, so the baseball team is practicing at the Rec Center field.

Rebuild milestone: 1,000 homes started

The Palisadian-Post reports that Pacific Palisades passed 1,000 postfire homes started on August 12, according to the Pali Rebuild Map. As of August 13, the map showed 35 primary residences completed with certificates of occupancy and more than 1,700 permit submissions.

Treat this as a recovery signal, not a household action item: the piece suggests residential momentum may influence commercial owners, but it does not itself change permitting requirements.

Decisions Affecting Palisades

Commercial-core pressure shifts to Gelson’s and Ralphs

A Palisadian-Post opinion piece argues that Palisades Village’s reopening should increase pressure on Gelson’s, Ralphs and officials involved in commercial recovery. It is advocacy, not an official action, but it reflects a live resident concern: grocery and daily-retail recovery still lags the reopening of Palisades Village.

Mayoral race rhetoric touches City Hall performance

A Westside Current debate report covers Mayor Karen Bass and Councilwoman Nithya Raman clashing over attendance, homelessness, policing and City Hall performance. Palisades relevance is indirect unless you are tracking the Sunday candidate meet-and-greet or citywide leadership’s effect on recovery execution.

Fire/Rebuild Watch

Construction-site theft remains a rebuild pressure point

KTLA reports that residents are using barricades, private security, neighborhood watch activity and camera-alert tools to protect rebuild sites. LAPD recorded eight burglaries in June in the area, with tools and equipment taken from storage containers, and Councilwoman Traci Park’s office said four additional officers have been assigned.

Practical takeaway: if you have an active site, the local concern is tool and equipment security, especially at night.

Architect and contractor selection get practical rebuild guides

Two Palisadian-Post rebuild pieces are worth saving if you are hiring now. One explains how to evaluate architects for a fire rebuild, including local experience, workload, proposal scope, permitting and construction-phase services. The other argues that contractor trust and communication matter as much as price because rebuilds involve months of problem-solving.

Claim and rebuild records: useful checklist, but private source

A private insurance-services page offers a claim and rebuild record checklist with links to California Department of Insurance, debris-removal and lender-disbursement resources. Treat it as a checklist prompt; for decisions, rely on your insurer, lender, public agencies and professional advice.
