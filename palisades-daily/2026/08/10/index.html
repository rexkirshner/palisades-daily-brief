Key Stories

CD11 update bundles infrastructure and recovery programming

Council District 11's Aug. 8 Palisades update is the practical read today. It covers LADWP water project scheduling, Palisades Basin No. 69 work on Avenida de Santa Ynez and Palisades Drive through Oct. 15, and recovery programming tied to taxes, resilience, and healing.

Resident takeaway: watch for slower traffic and one-lane merging around the basin project, while sidewalks, driveways, trash service, and deliveries are described as remaining available.

Pali High football is back home

Circling The News reports Pali High football has its field back after last year's postfire scramble for practice and game locations. Coach Dylen Smith says the team is excited to be back home, and the first game is scheduled against Crenshaw at 7 p.m. on Aug. 21.

Resident takeaway: this is less about standings than normalcy. A major school program is no longer operating as a displaced team.

Kumon rebuilds from the fire in Brentwood

The Palisadian-Post follows Tricia Cosentino, whose Kumon Math and Reading Center at 860 Via de la Paz was destroyed in the Palisades Fire. She opened a relocated center at 12381 Wilshire Boulevard in Brentwood on April 7, 2025, and says enrollment is now nearing prefire levels.

Resident takeaway: this is a useful school-and-family recovery story. The business survived by following displaced families, but a return to the Palisades is still uncertain.

Big Blue Bus adjusts Route 9 around Pali High

Big Blue Bus Route 9 lists an Aug. 9 service change for Pacific Palisades, with schedule adjustments for express service from Palisades Charter High School to Downtown Santa Monica to better align with the school bell schedule.

Resident takeaway: if your household uses Route 9 for Pali High or Santa Monica trips, check the updated schedule before assuming last year's timing still applies.

Decisions Affecting Palisades

Measure ULA exemption remains November-ballot background

The Real Deal recaps the November ballot measure that would give a one-time, five-year Measure ULA transfer-tax exemption for homes damaged or destroyed by the Palisades Fire. It also notes the measure followed a 13-1 City Council vote and would run from the time of the fire through January 2030.

Resident takeaway: this matters most if you may sell a fire-damaged or destroyed property, buy a burned lot, or follow rebuild incentives. There is no new resident action in today's report beyond tracking the November vote.

Fire/Rebuild Watch

Federal help gap remains a rebuild risk

Governing reports that 85% of people who lost homes in the January 2025 fires are still displaced, based on a Department of Angels report. It says the median gap between insurance and rebuild costs is more than $1 million for Malibu and Pacific Palisades homeowners.

Resident takeaway: this is background, but it frames why rebuilding remains financially impossible for many households even when permits and cleanup move forward.

Malibu's slower rebuild is a PCH-adjacent warning

Spectrum News reports Malibu has issued 88 building permits, while Palisades permits exceed 1,400 and Altadena has more than 1,000 homes in construction. Malibu officials point to steep terrain, eroding shoreline, bureaucracy, and expected rains as constraints.

Resident takeaway: this is not a Palisades permitting update, but it is useful context for PCH, coastal erosion, and hillside rebuild risk.

Soil safety is becoming part of rebuild due diligence

Propmodo looks at research around postfire soil contamination and rebuilding. The article says fire debris can leave heavy metals, asbestos, and other toxins on nearby properties, making safety questions broader than debris removal alone.

Resident takeaway: if you are rebuilding or buying near burn areas, soil and environmental testing questions are worth asking before construction assumptions harden.
