Key Stories

Farmers Market returns to Swarthmore

The farmers market reopened Wednesday after 19 months away, with vendors returning to Swarthmore Avenue and a temporary Wednesday 1:30 to 5:30 p.m. schedule. For residents back in town, this is both food access and a visible community-recovery milestone.

A Daily News account also says organizers chose Wednesdays because many vendors have Sunday commitments elsewhere.

Pali High rebuild timeline comes into focus

Palisades Charter High School’s permanent rebuild is moving toward a new classroom building, a Pali Academy building and restored athletic facilities, with construction slated to begin in early 2027 and occupancy projected for late 2028.

A back-to-school report says students began the 2026-27 school year on the Bowdoin Street campus, with instruction running out of 36 temporary bungalows on the baseball field while permanent work continues.

Residents respond to construction-site theft risk

A report on resident security efforts says some Palisades residents are using nighttime barricades and informal watch efforts around rebuild zones after construction-site thefts. LAPD has urged residents and contractors to lock up equipment, use trackers on expensive items, add lighting and cameras where possible, and check properties regularly.

The practical takeaway: if you are rebuilding, security planning is now part of the project budget and contractor conversation.

Palisades Towne Hall waits on permits

Palisades Towne Hall is still moving through permitting for a beer-and-wine garden and marketplace at 859 and 861 Via de la Paz. Founder Brian Garrett says the nonprofit has raised more than $420,000 and hopes to hold a one-day September event if permits allow.

Pali High homepage has back-to-school parent items

The Pali High website is carrying current parent-facing links, including a welcome-back webinar recording, ParentSquare registration, bus registration, environmental testing updates and the 2026-27 calendar. Useful if you need logistics more than reporting.

Decisions Affecting Palisades

PPCC drafts a two-year recovery strategy

The Pacific Palisades Community Council draft plan covers 2026 to 2028 and emphasizes recovery advocacy, civic engagement, public awareness, streamlined meetings and organizational capacity. This matters because PPCC is positioning itself as a convener and advocate during the rebuild.

NORC and PPCC launch follow-up recovery survey

PPCC and NORC launched a 15- to 20-minute survey to track resident priorities as recovery evolves. If you received the invitation, it is one of the cleaner ways to put household-level experience into the record.

Community Council candidacy dispute surfaces

Circling The News reports that a resident attorney questioned Richard Fink’s PPCC at-large candidacy, citing his State Parks role tied to Temescal Gateway Park and the Lachman/Palisades fire litigation context. Treat this as a watch item unless the candidate responds or PPCC takes a formal step.

Fire/Rebuild Watch

Commercial core recovery remains uneven

A Palisadian-Post interview says Rick Caruso is pressing other commercial property owners, including major grocery operators, to move faster on rebuilding and reopening. He also said he bought 1021 North Swarthmore Avenue and wants to bring a restaurant there.

Broader coverage from Insurance Journal and Bloomberg frames the Village reopening as a visible milestone but not proof that the whole recovery is moving evenly.

Rebuild permitting remains uneven for families

A Palisadian-Post views piece argues that families are experiencing very different permit timelines and calls for clearer points of contact, transparent timelines and earlier issue identification.

A separate Los Angeles permit-timeline guide says standard residential plan check can take 6 to 14 weeks and fire rebuild permits under emergency orders target 30 days for like-for-like submittals, with first-pass plan quality a major variable.

Insurance and financing pressure remains background

A Business Journal article summarizes continuing insurance, displacement and rebuild-cost gaps after the January 2025 fires. Useful context, but today’s local sources do not add a new resident deadline from that piece.
