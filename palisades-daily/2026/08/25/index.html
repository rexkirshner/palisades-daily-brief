Key Stories

Tax webinar for fire victims is the most immediate action item

Circling The News reports that Andrew Cereghino of Edward Jones is hosting a free webinar tonight, August 25, from 6 to 8 p.m. on “Tax Solutions for Pacific Palisades Fire Victims,” featuring CPA John Trapani. The practical issue is IRS Section 1033, which can let some owners defer taxes after destroyed property and insurance compensation if they reinvest within required timeframes. Read the item.

Candidate forum centers Palisades recovery in city and state races

At an August 23 Palisades Recovery Coalition forum, Nithya Raman and candidates for state Senate and Assembly seats discussed recovery governance, funding, accountability, and interagency coordination. The useful takeaway is not campaign theater: residents are getting direct access to candidates on rebuild-specific questions. Two more forums are planned for September 27 and October 18 at 11 a.m. at 15233 La Cruz Drive. Read the Palisadian-Post story.

Local business owners are reopening and signing leases

The Palisadian-Post reports that K Bakery is open in Palisades Village, Flour Pizzeria has signed for the former Noah’s Bagels space on Antioch, Ludus opened on Sunset, Loomey’s Toys is expected later this year, and Palisades General Store is preparing for Highlands Plaza. A separate Post story says Palisadian Jacqueline Chernov opened The Palisades Estates office at 100 West Channel Road. Read the business roundup and the West Channel office story.

Lake Shrine marks 75 years after surviving the fire

Self-Realization Fellowship Lake Shrine held its 75th anniversary celebration on August 22 after the event was postponed from 2025 because of the Palisades Fire. The site survived, but remediation and rebuilding continue, and leaders are discussing a future master plan after acquiring a one-acre parcel at 17250 Sunset Boulevard. Read the Palisadian-Post story.

Decisions Affecting Palisades

State wildfire-liability fight could affect fire victims and utility bills

PBS/AP reports that Gov. Gavin Newsom is seeking a deal to further shield utilities from financial trouble when their equipment sparks wildfires, while also aiming to stabilize electricity rates and pay survivors sooner. This is statewide, but relevant to Palisades because fire survivors, insurers, utilities, and ratepayers could all be affected by how liability is allocated. Read the PBS/AP story.

Candidate commitments are becoming part of the rebuild record

The Palisades forum story is worth saving because candidates discussed dedicated Palisades/Malibu recovery staffing, city trust, funding, and interagency coordination in front of residents. That creates a baseline for later accountability, even if it does not itself change policy today. Read the Palisadian-Post story.

Fire/Rebuild Watch

Permit delays may depend heavily on plan quality and response time

Circling The News reports that some builders are moving faster through permits because they submit more complete plans and answer city comments quickly. The practical takeaway: if you are early in design, ask architects and builders how they handle city comments, whether they use electronic filing or paper, and how fast they typically respond. Read the story.

Lower-cost housing losses remain a major unresolved rebuild issue

The same Circling The News piece says the Palisades lost mobile homes, apartments, and other lower-cost housing in the fire, and describes complicated responsibility between tenants, park owners, state housing authority, civil courts, and coastal housing law. This is a “read if affected” item, not a quick-fix story. Read the story.

Construction-site burglary coverage continues

A Yahoo/California Post item repeats that some residents have organized nighttime blockades or neighborhood-watch efforts around rebuild sites after construction-area break-ins. This was already a leading issue recently, so the only useful takeaway today is to keep securing tools, containers, and active sites. Read the story.
