Key Stories

Residents organize security around rebuild sites

New York Post and KTLA both report residents are blocking streets at night and hiring private security after construction properties were targeted.

Why Rex should care: this is a practical rebuild risk, not just crime coverage. If nearby lots have tools, materials, temporary utilities, or open framing, this is worth discussing with neighbors and contractors.

Rotary rebuild activity is becoming a local civic asset

Circling The News reports the Palisades Rotary Club grew from nine members after the fire to 31 members by August 2026, and highlighted grants for local projects including Pali High tables and benches, Rotary corner landscaping, Lowe Family YMCA rebuilding, and six small business grants.

Why it matters: this is one of the more concrete examples of local civic capacity rebuilding after the fire.

Pali High football is back on campus

The Palisadian-Post previewed Pali High’s season opener against Crenshaw, noting it was the Dolphins’ first on-campus game since Nov. 8, 2024.

This is mostly community-life context now that the game date has passed, but the larger point is useful: school routines and campus identity are continuing to return.

Decisions Affecting Palisades

Insurance questions remain unresolved

The United Policyholders writeup of the Palisades Recovery Coalition insurance town hall says residents raised concerns about underinsurance, claim delays, smoke-damage payments, future availability of coverage, condos, HOAs, and the California Fair Plan.

Practical takeaway: no clear resident fix emerged in the writeup. The most concrete legislative item mentioned was SB 495, described as a proposal to require insurers to pay wildfire survivors 100% of contents coverage without a detailed inventory list, but the article says it would not apply retroactively.

Old eldercare land-use letter resurfaces, but it is not new action

Palisades News republished or resurfaced a PPCC letter about the Highlands eldercare facility, originally tied to older land-use proceedings. It clarifies PPCC’s past position that an eldercare use was appropriate, while not endorsing every design or legal finding.

This is background unless a current hearing or appeal appears.

Fire/Rebuild Watch

Rebuild math gets more precise

Circling The News distinguishes several numbers that are often blurred together: 5,261 single-family homes destroyed including condos, 5,427 CalFire destroyed “Single Residence” inspection records, and 3,860 distinct parcels in Pacific Palisades.

The piece also reports 2,238 applications received for unique addresses, 1,560 permits issued, and 33 certificates of occupancy on the L.A. City dashboard. For Rex, the useful takeaway is that “homes destroyed” and “lots available to rebuild” are not the same metric.

Empty-lot rebuild model is being pitched

The same Circling The News story describes the Uplifters Foundation plan to buy 60 lots over two years and place 2,600-square-foot houses on them, renting before selling and reinvesting charitable proceeds locally.

This is worth watching if it affects lot pricing, rebuild speed, or whether burned parcels become family homes versus speculative inventory.
