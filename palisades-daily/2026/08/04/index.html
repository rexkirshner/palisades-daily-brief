Key Stories

PPCC election gives residents a real civic checklist

The Palisadian-Post reports 17 candidates are running for PPCC area and at-large seats. Five of eight area races are contested; Chris Spitz and Richard Fink are running for at-large representative.

Why it matters: voting runs Aug. 21-Sept. 4, results are due no later than Sept. 10, and displaced residents who intend to return can vote by declaring that intent. Read the statements before the Aug. 13 forum and send questions directly to candidates.

Theatre Palisades starts feasibility work on rebuilding Pierson Playhouse

Theatre Palisades told members it has begun early feasibility work for rebuilding the Pierson Playhouse, including surveys of the site and foundation. Its parking lot will likely be rented to LADWP for undergrounding power-line staging.

Practical takeaway: the theater is not back home yet, but it is operating. Its production of “Kimberly Akimbo” is scheduled Aug. 14-30 at Odyssey Theatre in West Los Angeles.

Pali High tennis leadership changes after postfire scramble

Circling The News reports Rob Silvers has been named Pali High tennis co-head coach after helping keep the program running after the fire. He coordinated parents, temporary practice sites, and replacement gear while longtime coach Bud Kling dealt with losing his home.

Why it matters: this is a school-recovery story, not just an athletics note. It shows how Pali High programs are being held together through temporary locations, facilities coordination, and volunteer-heavy leadership.

Decisions Affecting Palisades

Farmers proposes more small-business coverage in wildfire-stressed areas, with higher rates

Insurance Journal reports Farmers filed a plan to write more than 1,500 new business owner and business property policies in wildfire-stressed California areas over two years, with an average statewide increase of more than 15%. The proposed effective date is Feb. 1, 2027.

Why it matters: this is business coverage, not homeowner coverage, but Palisades businesses and property owners should watch whether availability improves enough to offset higher pricing.

Senator Allen says he is not affiliated with Uplifters Foundation

Palisades News reports Sen. Ben Allen’s office said he never joined the Uplifters Foundation board and is not affiliated with the group. The clarification follows the nonprofit’s proposal to buy burned lots and build roughly 60 below-market “Heritage Homes.”

Practical takeaway: treat the Uplifters proposal as unsettled until governance, financing, and public claims are clearer.

Fire/Rebuild Watch

County recovery bulletin points to furnishings help and a rebuilding workshop

The LA County Coordinated Joint Information Center bulletin points Palisades and Eaton fire survivors to A Sense of Home for essential furnishings and home goods. It also lists a Rebuilding Together virtual workshop on the Unified Permit Process for Aug. 14 from 9 to 11 a.m.

Why it matters: if you are rebuilding or helping someone re-house after the fire, this is more actionable than another process story.

Malibu coastal erosion remains a PCH-adjacent recovery issue

PublicCEO reports Malibu is coordinating with Caltrans on coastal erosion along PCH where beachfront properties were destroyed in the Palisades Fire.

Why it matters: this is Malibu-focused, but it affects the same coastal corridor Palisades residents use and is part of the broader postfire infrastructure picture.

Commercial rebuild guidance is useful but vendor-sourced

A builder’s rebuilding guide emphasizes early checks on debris clearance, insurance scope, permit pathway, Coastal Zone status, utilities, and contractor availability. Use it as a checklist prompt, not an official source.
