Key Stories

Rebuild map shows slow but steady progress

The Pali Rebuild Map says July had the highest number of permit submissions this year, narrowly, and reports 1,705 parcels with rebuild permit submissions as of Aug. 5, equal to 41.5% of destroyed parcels.

The more useful resident takeaway: completed homes are still a lagging indicator. Permit submissions, shovel-ready permits and construction starts are better signs of whether neighbors and businesses should expect a returning community.

Palisades Village reopening gets a detailed schedule

Palisades Village plans to reopen Aug. 15 with a 10:30 a.m. ribbon cutting, stores and restaurants opening at 11 a.m., family activities and entertainment through the day, and an evening finale at 8 p.m. Complimentary parking is planned through Sept. 30.

The center is described as reopening 99% leased, with returning tenants and new restaurants and retailers. For residents, this is less about shopping and more about the return of a walkable center of gravity.

Temescal Gateway Park becomes a practical recovery bright spot

Temescal Gateway Park has reopened meaningful access after the fire, including rebuilt stairs, three new bridges along Sunset Trail, widened paths and cleared brush. Rivas Trail and Sunset Trail are open; the waterfall trail remains closed.

If you use the park, the practical point is simple: parts of Temescal are again usable, but don’t assume the full pre-fire trail system is back.

Decisions Affecting Palisades

Measure ULA fire exemption heads to voters, with condo and HOA concerns

Multiple outlets report that the L.A. City Council voted 13-1 to put a November ballot measure before voters that would exempt qualifying sales of Palisades Fire-damaged or destroyed homes from Measure ULA for five years from the date of the fire.

Read one of these if you need the mechanics: Palisades News, LAist, NBC Los Angeles, or Patch.

The new local angle worth noting is Circling the News’ condo and HOA framing: some condo and townhome owners may face HOA dissolution rather than a voluntary sale, and ballot wording could matter for whether voters understand that distinction.

Fire/Rebuild Watch

Beacon Boxes raise a concrete wildfire-response planning question

Circling the News reports on “Beacon Boxes,” which provide maps, hydrant locations, turnarounds, pools and other local fire-response information for outside fire crews.

The practical takeaway is not that the idea is proven for the Palisades, but that it is a specific preparedness tool residents and local groups may want to evaluate before the next major fire response.

Federal wildfire recovery money remains unresolved

Gov. Gavin Newsom’s office says California is still waiting on federal long-term wildfire recovery funding for communities including Pacific Palisades, Altadena and Malibu.

This is background unless you are tracking public funding streams, schools, infrastructure or recovery-program timing. It does not create a resident deadline today.

Around The Web

Rebuild spending advice is useful background

The Palisadian-Post has a practical rebuild-design piece on where to invest versus save. The useful rule of thumb: prioritize hard-to-change fundamentals like layout, light, storage, wiring, windows and doors over finishes that can be upgraded later.

Insurance pressure remains a national wildfire backdrop

A national insurance piece from Straight Arrow News uses Spokane and other wildfire regions to discuss underinsurance, rising premiums and insurer pullbacks. For Palisades residents, it is context rather than new local guidance.
