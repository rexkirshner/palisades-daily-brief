Key Stories

PPCC forum tonight; voting opens Aug. 21

The Pacific Palisades Community Council candidate forum is set for 6 p.m. tonight after a brief board meeting. Contested races include Areas 1 through 5 and the at-large seat.

A Palisades News report says electronic voting begins at 8 a.m. Aug. 21 and ends at 8 p.m. Sept. 4 through the council website; it is the only voting method described.

Palisades Village reopening is now a detailed Saturday calendar item

Palisadian-Post reports Palisades Village will reopen Saturday, Aug. 15, with a 10:30 a.m. ribbon cutting, 11 a.m. store and restaurant openings, retailer activations, pop-ups, live entertainment, tastings, family activities, and an 8 p.m. finale.

The tenant list includes returning restaurants such as Porta Via, Angelini, and Blue Ribbon Sushi, plus K Bakery, Elyse Walker, Alphabet Bar & Grill, and Spacca Tutto. Complimentary parking is available through Sept. 30.

ABC7 adds that 99% of businesses are leased, about 70% are expected to open Saturday, and the rest over the next few months.

Pali High enters another recovery school year

Circling The News reports Pali High starts its 65th school year with Dr. Pamela Magee beginning her 15th year leading the school. Temporary classrooms are on the baseball field while bungalows are rebuilt.

The practical takeaway: enrollment is down from about 3,000 to 2,500, core staff mostly stayed, and the school is adding International Baccalaureate classes. A separate Palisadian-Post history piece is worth skimming if you want local school context, not urgent news.

Pali High runner broke a 42-year-old City Section record

Circling The News profiles Ernesto Jalomo, who transferred to Pali High, set five school records, broke the Los Angeles City Section 1600-meter record in 4:08.54, and committed to run at UC Berkeley.

This is a local sports bright spot rather than a resident action item.

Decisions Affecting Palisades

Density and parking remain a core rebuild fight

Two Circling The News opinion pieces argue that density, parking, mixed-use redevelopment, and the cost of rebuilding small commercial spaces will shape what the Palisades becomes after the fire: Part 1 focuses on density, while Part 2 focuses on mom-and-pop stores.

Treat these as framing pieces, not official action. The resident takeaway is that rebuilding commercial life may collide with parking limits, construction costs, and state housing rules.

Measure ULA exemption remains ballot background

The Registry reports the City Council voted 13-1 to put a Palisades fire-related Measure ULA exemption before November voters. The available text is limited after the opening summary, so this remains background unless you are planning a sale or tracking the ballot measure.

Fire/Rebuild Watch

Private contractor guidance flags rebuild contract risk

A private builder post says cost-plus contracts are prohibited for Pacific Palisades fire rebuilds and urges homeowners to verify requirements directly with the Contractors State License Board. Because this is private guidance, use it as a prompt to check official rules before signing.

A private permitting service also summarizes fire rebuild permitting paths for Pacific Palisades, Altadena, and Malibu, including agency differences, documentation, fee waivers, and fast-track programs. Useful as a checklist, not a substitute for city or county confirmation.

Disaster-recovery mortgage pilot is worth watching if financing is your bottleneck

A press-release item says Lendistry Home Loans launched a disaster recovery mortgage pilot for homeowners displaced by the 2025 LA wildfires. The available article text is mostly publication framing, so treat it as a lead to verify directly before relying on it.
